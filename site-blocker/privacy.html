FocusGate privacy policy

Publisher: Matheus da Silveira Mello
Contact: matheus.mello.developer@gmail.com
Effective date: 8 November 2026

Privacy notice

FocusGate is a free browser extension that blocks distracting websites according to settings you choose. It keeps those settings in your browser and does not send them, or any other information, to us or to anyone else. FocusGate makes no network requests and has no accounts, purchases, advertising, analytics, telemetry or uninstall survey.

What FocusGate stores in your browser

FocusGate saves the following in its extension storage in your browser profile. FocusGate does not sync it to other devices.

  • Your sites: the domains you add, optionally with a section such as youtube.com/shorts.
  • Your rhythm: weekly windows, on/off settings, and the end time of a focus session you start.
  • Your reminder: the note you write, up to 500 characters, shown on the blocked page.
  • Daily counts: for each of the latest 30 days with activity, how many times FocusGate's calm blocked page opened. A count has no site name, page address, page title or time of day attached.
  • Lock challenge: while you are changing settings in lock mode, the random 60-character phrase and the time the challenge started. These are held in temporary session storage, deleted after a successful unlock, and cleared when the browser closes.

What FocusGate does not collect

  • A list of the pages you visit. Your browser applies FocusGate's rules itself while it loads pages, so FocusGate is not told the addresses of the pages you visit.
  • Page content. FocusGate does not run scripts on websites and cannot read page text, forms or passwords.
  • Information sent over the network. FocusGate sends nothing. Its pages are built so they cannot make network connections.

"Block this site"

When you click "Block this site" in FocusGate's toolbar window, it reads the current tab's address once, keeps only the site's name (for example, example.com) as a new entry, and discards the rest. It reads nothing if you don't click.

Optional site access and the calm blocked page

Blocking works without any site access; your browser then shows its own blocked-page message. When you add a site, the browser asks whether FocusGate may access it. If you allow it, FocusGate uses that access only to send blocked visits to its calm page. You can also allow the calm page for all sites, which allow-list mode uses. You can decline or later remove site access in your browser's extension settings; blocking continues either way.

The calm page's address includes the blocked site's name (or "all-sites" in allow-list mode) so the page can say what was blocked. Your browser may list that address in its own history, as it does for other pages you open. FocusGate cannot read your browser history.

Your choices

Edit or remove sites, windows and your reminder in FocusGate's Settings page. Uninstalling FocusGate removes its stored data from your browser. Copies of your browser profile, device backups and organization-managed browser policies are outside FocusGate's control. Because we never receive your data, we cannot view, export or delete it for you. If you allow FocusGate in private windows, calm-page openings there add to the same local daily counts.

Other services

Your browser, the extension store and the websites you visit have their own privacy practices. Opening this page or emailing us is separate from using the extension; FocusGate never opens external pages by itself. If you email support, we and the email providers involved receive what you send. Please leave out browsing details you consider sensitive.

We will update this notice, with a new effective date, before any change to what FocusGate stores or sends.
