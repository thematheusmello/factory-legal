QuickType privacy policy

Publisher: Matheus da Silveira Mello
Contact: matheus.mello.developer@gmail.com
Effective date: 8 November 2026
Applies to: QuickType – Text Snippet Expander 0.2.0 for Firefox desktop and Chrome/Edge.

Summary. QuickType keeps your snippets in your browser. It runs only on websites you enable. It never reads password, one-time-code or payment card fields. It makes no network requests of its own, and its publisher receives none of your data. The one optional feature that sends data off your device is browser sync. It is off until you turn it on, and you are asked first.

Your snippet library. The snippets you create or import (name, shortcut, text, folder) and your settings are stored in your browser's extension storage. Import reads a file you choose. Export writes a JSON file you control.

Text you type on websites. QuickType runs only on sites you enable from its toolbar button, or on all sites if you choose that in the studio. On those sites, to detect a shortcut, it looks at no more than the 65 characters before your cursor in the field you are typing in. Those characters are not stored, logged or transmitted. QuickType never reads or expands password fields, one-time-code fields, or payment card fields (number, security code, expiry, cardholder). When a snippet is inserted, the website receives that text like anything else you type, and handles it under its own policies.

Clipboard. Only if you allow clipboard placeholders, QuickType reads your clipboard at the moment a snippet containing {clipboard} expands. It inserts the text and does not keep it. Answers you type into {input:…} fill-in prompts are also used once and not saved.

Optional browser sync.

  • When it is off: sync is off by default, and nothing is sent.
  • Asking first: when you turn it on, Firefox asks for your permission to share personally identifying information and personal communications. In Chrome and Edge, QuickType shows the same explanation and asks you to confirm. If you decline, nothing is written to sync.
  • What is shared: your whole snippet library (names, shortcuts, text and folders), plus a snippet count and a random revision code, is copied to your browser's sync storage. Snippets often contain names, addresses and message text, which is why consent is requested.
  • Who receives it: if your browser account syncs extensions, your browser provider stores the copy and delivers it to your other devices. That is Mozilla through your Mozilla account in Firefox, Google through your Google Account in Chrome, and Microsoft through your Microsoft account in Edge. The provider's own terms and privacy policy govern that service. QuickType's publisher never receives your snippets.
  • What is never synced: settings, enabled sites, typed text, clipboard contents and fill-in answers.
  • Merging: turning sync on keeps snippets already synced from your other devices. Afterwards, the most recent change from any device replaces the library on the others.
  • Turning it off: turning sync off, or withdrawing the permission in your browser, removes QuickType's synced copy. The removal reaches your provider the next time your browser syncs. Copies already on your other devices, browser backups and your exported files are not erased.
  • Advice: do not store passwords, payment card numbers or health details in snippets you sync.

Your choices. Delete snippets in the studio. Remove enabled sites in the studio or in your browser's extension settings. Turn clipboard placeholders off at any time. Turn sync off. Uninstalling the extension deletes its local data, subject to your browser's behavior. Delete exported files yourself. For privacy questions, contact matheus.mello.developer@gmail.com. If you write to us, we receive what you choose to send; we keep support emails only as long as needed to answer and follow up, and delete them within 12 months.

No tracking. QuickType contains no analytics, advertising, payment or tracking code, and loads no remote code.

Changes. We will update this notice, with a new effective date, before releasing a version that handles data differently.
